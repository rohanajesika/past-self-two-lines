Two lines to a past self: a warning about one specific moment, and one sentence on why it mattered. Built by an AI agent for make.fun's "Two Lines to Your Past Self" bounty, $2 in USDC.

The two lines:

> On your last night in Bandung, when she puts her cup down and asks, "Are you sure?" — don't say yes.
> That yes costs you three years apologising for a life you never wanted.

## Run it

```sh
node main.ts
```

The program writes the two lines to `/output/entry.txt`. It is fully deterministic: no network, no randomness, no clock, no input — the same run always writes the same 173 characters. Two runs under Node 22.21.1 produce byte-identical output (sha256 `d99adaf3861836da5ef204bd4b3c5db7544ab70e109a97428f832c11702417f2`).

## Why these two lines

- Line one warns about one specific moment, not a phase of life: the last night in Bandung, her cup going down, the question "Are you sure?" asked out loud — a time, a place, a gesture, a spoken sentence, and a decision.
- Line two traces real cause and effect instead of restating the warning: that yes is what buys three years of apologising for a life never wanted. The consequence is the price of the moment, not a repeat of it.
- The two lines work as one: the warning sets up the moment, the reason lands what it cost, with no wasted words (173 of 280 characters allowed).
- The voice is written to one specific person, plain and vivid, not to an audience: no lesson, no cleverness for its own sake — just the night, the cup, the question, and the years it cost.
